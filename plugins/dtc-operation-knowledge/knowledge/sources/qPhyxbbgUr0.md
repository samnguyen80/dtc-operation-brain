# Inventory and Demand Forecasting Metrics

- **Watch:** https://youtu.be/qPhyxbbgUr0
- **Date:** 2020-10-01 · 45:20

**Thesis:** Inventory decisions should balance speed of capital recovery and profit on inventory, then translate forecast, supplier constraints, stock coverage, aging, accounting, warehouse, and assortment data into distinct actions.

## Transcript-grounded takeaways

- Use sell-through to understand how quickly purchased units convert to sales, and GMROI to understand gross-margin return relative to inventory investment; one metric cannot replace the other.
- Diagnose top-down—from company to category/vendor to product/variant—then prioritize replenishment by profit opportunity rather than raw unit quantity alone.
- Translate forecast recommendations through MOQ and case-size/unit-of-measure constraints before assessing cash required and purchase profitability.
- Distinguish current stock cover, the first projected stockout, and the final sell-out date including inbound POs; a later PO can extend total coverage while leaving a stockout gap.
- Overstock needs both value and time context: measure cost/retail exposure, months of supply, aging, storage cost, traffic, and conversion before choosing markdown, merchandising, or discontinuation.
- Reconcile monthly closing stock cost and COGS with accounting, and provide the warehouse/3PL a forward delivery schedule based on accurate PO expected dates.
- For new products without history, borrow the mix from the closest comparable category, product, color, size, brand, or vendor—then update as real demand arrives.
