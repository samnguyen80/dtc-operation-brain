# Cash Conversion Cycle: The Metric EVERY Ecommerce Owner Should Understand

- **Channel:** LedgerGurus: Ecommerce Accounting Experts
- **Watch:** https://youtu.be/F-Y1cubdesE
- **Date:** 2025-12-30 · 8:02

**Thesis:** Profitability does not guarantee liquidity; the cash conversion cycle makes visible how long cash remains trapped between supplier payment, inventory sale, channel payout, and vendor terms.

## Transcript-grounded takeaways

- DTC growth is usually prefunded: the brand buys inventory before sale and may wait for channel or wholesale payment after recognizing revenue.
- `DIO = average inventory / COGS × period days`; long DIO can come from overbuying, SKU proliferation, lead time, and slow products hidden inside bundles.
- `DSO = accounts receivable / revenue × period days`; the speaker contrasts fast DTC payouts with slower marketplace and wholesale terms. Shopify at about **two business days**, Amazon at about **two weeks**, and wholesale at **30/60/90 days** are **[claimed]** examples, not universal platform promises.
- `DPO = accounts payable / COGS × period days`; longer negotiated supplier terms delay cash outflow, while prepayment compresses DPO.
- `CCC = DIO + DSO - DPO`. Dell and Costco are offered as examples of near-zero or negative cycles; Costco selling inventory in about **10 days** against **30-day supplier terms** is **[claimed]**.
- The speaker commonly sees ecommerce CCC of at least **90 days** and some brands holding **180–240 inventory days** **[claimed]**. These observations are not category-adjusted benchmarks.
- Operational levers are better supplier terms, liquidation of bad inventory, tighter reorder points, and explicit financing of the remaining cycle—not merely more sales.
