# Amazon Logistics and Micro-Fulfillment Masterclass

- **Watch:** https://youtu.be/VoFp2fRfAQk
- **Date:** 2023-07-08 · 47:19
- **Channel:** eCom Logistics Podcast

**Thesis:** Omnichannel fulfillment works backward from assortment, customer promise, and inventory turns; decentralization helps only when the brand can replenish and control one coherent inventory system.

## Transcript-grounded takeaways

- The episode maps 2023-era Amazon options across Vendor Central/direct fulfillment and Seller Central/FBA, merchant fulfilled, Seller Fulfilled Prime, and Amazon Local. Program rules and names are time-sensitive; use the map only as historical context (12:34–25:39).
- Assortment must be chosen per channel. The speaker recommends keeping core high-volume items in Amazon's network while preserving outside inventory and other fulfillment paths so one channel does not control every unit (15:14–22:46).
- Prime-like programs impose operational obligations beyond printing a label: order cycle time, carrier scan, delivery promise, and location coverage all have to align. The speaker cites cycle times as short as **six hours** in some direct-fulfillment cases **[claimed]** (17:01–20:52).
- Micro-fulfillment is forward-deployed inventory in smaller local nodes; it is not synonymous with 15-minute delivery. The business described six buildings reaching **85% next-day and 100% two-day coverage** **[claimed]** (36:51–39:41).
- The economics depend on turns, MOQ, replenishment lead time, density, building cube, and last-mile options. The operator targets less than **eight weeks on hand** at each node **[claimed]**; this is a vendor operating target, not a general benchmark (39:41–42:32).
- Automation cannot repair bad assortment or dead inventory. If cube enters faster than it leaves, denser automation only hides the inventory problem temporarily (41:35–43:24).
- Splitting identical inventory pools among separate 3PLs for Shopify, Amazon, and Walmart is described as a fast way to lose control. Keep common inventory available to multiple programs where the systems and channel rules allow it (43:24–45:26).
