# Supply Chain Planning for $2.4M of Orders [claimed]

- **Watch:** https://youtu.be/oAU-1LwPzbY
- **Date:** 2020-10-15 · 22:31
- **Channel:** EcommOps

**Thesis:** Marketing cannot scale past physical capacity; a short demand window requires factory capacity, backup supply, warehouse labor, QC, shipping capacity, and customer communication to be committed before the sales spike.

## Transcript-grounded takeaways

- In the first case, demand repeatedly exceeded the next batch and marketing had to slow despite attractive performance. Revenue of roughly **$225,000 in 40 days**, margin near **25%**, and foregone upside are all speaker-reported **[claimed]** (1:55–4:54).
- For the second launch, the operator contacted more than ten factories, reserved one factory's daily capacity, and kept two backup factories. Volumes of **10,000–15,000 units per day** and nearly **100 workers** are case-study claims **[claimed]** (6:39–10:31).
- Stock is only half the constraint. Fulfillment requires space, trained labor, receiving, pick/pack processes, carrier capacity, and QC; these resources do not expand instantly when ad spend rises (11:24–13:22).
- The dedicated QC team reportedly rejected about **1%** of inbound units before shipment; the seller later reported defect requests below **0.1%** **[claimed]**. The causal impact was not independently audited (11:24–16:07).
- Customer communication was treated as part of operations: delivery expectations were lengthened during disruption, while a faster but more expensive line was chosen because the product margin could support it (13:22–15:10).
- The speaker reports **$2.4M sales in 27 days**, refunds below **5%**, and net margin near **30%** **[claimed]**. These extraordinary event-driven results should illustrate capacity coordination, not serve as a forecast (14:14–16:07).
- The closing doctrine is to plan before peak, accept explicit inventory risk only when demand evidence and unit economics justify it, and require the agent to state capacity limits before the brand oversells (16:07–19:43).
