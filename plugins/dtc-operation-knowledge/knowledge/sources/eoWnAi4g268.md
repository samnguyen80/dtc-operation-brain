# Open-To-Buy Planning

- **Watch:** https://youtu.be/eoWnAi4g268
- **Date:** 2020-11-19 · 55:22

**Thesis:** Open-to-buy converts planned revenue and target forward stock cover into a purchasing budget, then reconciles existing stock, inbound POs, markdowns, margins, seasonality, and replenishable assortment before item-level buying.

## Transcript-grounded takeaways

- OTB is useful when assortment turns quickly, new products lack SKU history, revenue goals are aggressive, or seasonality makes item-level extrapolation unreliable.
- Start bottom-up by mutually exclusive category, vendor, or brand groups; top-down allocation can double count overlapping classifications or hide local overstock.
- The basic monthly control is: opening stock plus scheduled receipts, less planned sales/markdown effects, compared with the closing stock required to enter the next period at target forward cover.
- Plan at retail value for an apples-to-apples view, then convert the purchase budget to cost using planned margin and to units using average selling price; markdowns reduce inventory retail value and may require more units to reach the same revenue.
- OTB recommends what should be received in a month. If lead time is not modeled directly, planners must shift the buying decision earlier and aggregate the needed receipt periods manually.
- Existing POs can force overstock even when the desired cover target is lower; the system should surface that constraint instead of producing a negative purchase recommendation.
- Clean the planning population: exclude non-replenishable seasonal items, gift cards, dummy inventory, overlaps, and bad retail values before trusting the budget.
