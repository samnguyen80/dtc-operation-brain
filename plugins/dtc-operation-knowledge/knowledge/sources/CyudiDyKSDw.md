# Inventory Landed Costs Explained | What's Included and How to Calculate It

- **Channel:** LedgerGurus: Ecommerce Accounting Experts
- **Watch:** https://youtu.be/CyudiDyKSDw
- **Date:** 2023-11-15 · 3:13

**Thesis:** Supplier price is only buy cost; product profitability and pricing require the full cost needed to place a sellable unit in the warehouse.

## Transcript-grounded takeaways

- Buy cost is the supplier's product price. Landed cost adds the costs incurred from supplier to the warehouse-ready state.
- The cost chain can include origin transport, export/import customs, duties and taxes, international freight, insurance, destination inland transport, receiving, and additional packaging.
- Accurate landed cost supports margin analysis, product-level profitability, pricing, sourcing negotiations, and comparison of suppliers or routes.
- The worked example begins with **$10,000** of buy cost and adds **$500** origin transport, **$1,000** export customs, **$2,000** international freight, **$5,000** destination customs/fees, **$1,000** destination transport, and **$500** receiving **[claimed example]**.
- Cost capture needs an allocation policy by shipment and SKU; a single total that never reaches unit cost leaves reported gross margin overstated.
