# Why Demand Forecasting Matters

- **Watch:** https://youtu.be/n25p5tKMg2A
- **Date:** 2024-11-13 · 12:54

**Thesis:** Forecasting is not merely a sales estimate; it is the shared demand signal that lets suppliers, production, inventory, warehouse, logistics, pricing, promotion, and product teams prepare coherently.

## Transcript-grounded takeaways

- Separate long-range strategic forecasts, annual/mid-range budgets, and short-range monthly forecasts; each horizon supports different commitments and should not be forced into one number.
- Forecast quality affects customer availability, supplier raw materials and capacity, production scheduling, safety stock, obsolescence, warehouse labor, transport capacity, and product phase-in/phase-out.
- Safety stock reflects average demand, demand variation, lead time, and lead-time variation; better demand and lead-time information can release working capital.
- Share promotion and price-change plans upstream because they can pull orders forward or create demand spikes that historical averages will miss.
- A credible forecast strengthens supplier negotiation and relationships by helping the supplier plan its own people, machines, and materials.
- Warehouse demand should be translated into units, order lines, labor, containers, and space so distribution capacity is planned rather than surprised.
