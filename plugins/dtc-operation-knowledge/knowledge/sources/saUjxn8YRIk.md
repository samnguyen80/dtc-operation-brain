# Your Inventory Numbers Are Wrong — with Abir Syed, eCom CFO

- **Watch:** https://youtu.be/saUjxn8YRIk
- **Date:** 2026-04-13 · 28:15

**Thesis:** Inventory must remain reconcilable across physical quantity, inventory-management value, and accounting value from PO through deposit, goods in transit, receipt, manufacturing, transfer, sale, and shrinkage.

## Transcript-grounded takeaways

- A supplier deposit is not on-hand inventory; track it separately until ownership transfers and the goods move into a goods-in-transit state.
- Capitalize estimated freight, customs, and other landed costs so unit cost and COGS are not understated while invoices lag.
- On receipt, perform a three-way match among the PO, goods-received record, and supplier invoice; investigate quantity, price, damage, or short-shipment mismatches before payment and system updates.
- When the final freight bill differs from the accrual, update the cost of remaining inventory and route the sold portion through a COGS catch-up rather than silently rewriting closed periods.
- Manufacturing consumes raw materials and produces finished goods; the IMS and accounting ledger should reflect the same movement, including work in progress or goods received not invoiced when applicable.
- Transfers between locations do not change total inventory value, but goods in transit must remain visible or the IMS, 3PL counts, and ledger will appear to disagree.
- Lost, stolen, damaged, or expired units require both a quantity adjustment and a value adjustment; periodic physical counts surface silent shrinkage.
