# 5 Reasons Profit Lies Hidden in Inventory [Why Profitable Brands Still Feel Cash-Poor]

- **Channel:** LedgerGurus: Ecommerce Accounting Experts
- **Watch:** https://youtu.be/CzLu6UlYiZE
- **Date:** 2026-03-25 · 58:35

**Thesis:** Inventory accuracy is an operating design problem that appears in the financials; establish a baseline, close ownership and SOP gaps, then choose technology that reflects the real purchasing-to-fulfillment flow.

## Transcript-grounded takeaways

- Five recurring failure modes are unexplained margin swings, omitted landed costs, spreadsheets outgrown by complexity, split ownership between units and dollars, and no monthly reality check.
- The recommended sequence is baseline first, structural repair second, tooling third. Baseline questions cover quantity tracking, inventory valuation, current product costs, and whether accounting is accrual-based.
- Inventory remains an asset until a sale/fulfillment event recognizes COGS. Receipts, transfers, adjustments, samples, returns, production, and fulfillment therefore need explicit operational and accounting treatment.
- Clear decision rights precede software: name the owner of product-cost updates and adjustments, document receiving and movement SOPs, and run regular physical or cycle counts.
- Landed-cost allocation should match the cost driver—equal, quantity, volume, or subtotal—not a default rule. A case where helmets absorbed container cost intended for bikes changed a unit cost from about **$2 to $300** **[claimed]**.
- The speakers argue for an inventory system that joins purchase orders, landed costs, locations, channel stock, pick/pack, COGS, and accounting. Finale is said to sync channels and QuickBooks every **five minutes** **[claimed]**.
- Automation can also amplify mistakes: incorrect mappings can double-count freight or tariffs by expensing them immediately and again through capitalized inventory/COGS.
