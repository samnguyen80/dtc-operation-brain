# Why Your Inventory Reports Don't Match Reality

- **Channel:** LedgerGurus: Ecommerce Accounting Experts
- **Watch:** https://youtu.be/Te8opeUSGfw
- **Date:** 2026-02-11 · 8:45

**Thesis:** Inventory accounting errors usually originate in unrecorded operational events; trust requires physical counts, explainable margin movement, current landed costs, and reconciled SKU identities.

## Transcript-grounded takeaways

- The opening case describes a **$5 million** seller receiving a **$300,000** year-end inventory adjustment **[claimed]**. The point is that accounting cannot reconstruct damage, samples, returns, theft, bundling, or obsolescence that operations never records.
- The speaker describes roughly **20 failure points** between purchase and financial reporting **[claimed]**. The durable principle is that every inventory state change must create an auditable event.
- Unexplained gross-margin volatility is a diagnostic signal. Promotions and write-offs may explain a change; absent a documented business event, investigate COGS and inventory data before trusting the report.
- A physical-to-system count is the second test. The speaker treats an adjustment over **10% of inventory value** as a serious operational warning and recommends starting with the top **20 SKUs** **[claimed]**; these are heuristics, not universal control limits.
- Buy cost is not landed cost. The example moves from a **$5 supplier price** to a **$13 warehouse-ready cost** after freight, tariffs, inland transport, insurance, and packaging **[claimed]**.
- Common drift sources are damaged or missing units, unlogged samples, lost returns, channel-specific duplicate SKUs, stale landed costs, negative stock, and spreadsheets that no longer match system complexity.
- The proposed weekly diagnostic is: inspect six months of gross-margin behavior, count the top SKUs, and audit landed cost for the highest-revenue products. All thresholds should be set from the brand's own materiality and risk.
