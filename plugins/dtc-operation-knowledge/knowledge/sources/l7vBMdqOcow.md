# True Classic: Turn Customer Support Into a Profit Center

- **Channel:** DTC Podcast
- **Watch:** https://youtu.be/l7vBMdqOcow
- **Date:** 2024-05-06 · 41:31

**Thesis:** CX can operate as a conversion, retention, and product-intelligence function—not merely a ticket queue—when repetitive contacts are automated, agents remain available for judgment-heavy conversations, and customer feedback is routed back into the business.

## Transcript-grounded takeaways

- Support is often the first human contact with a DTC brand. Sizing advice, styling help, and recovery after delivery failures can affect both initial conversion and whether a customer returns.
- True Classic routes customer language and stories back into paid marketing, retention, and product decisions; its support team is positioned as a continuous voice-of-customer sensor.
- The company says its AI helpdesk partially resolves or fully deflects routine work, avoided the usual Q4 backlog, and allowed a smaller support team. The attributed revenue increase was over **$3 million** **[claimed]**.
- Satisfaction is surveyed on automated and human interactions. The speaker reports roughly **94% CSAT on deflected conversations** and about **98% collective CSAT** **[claimed]**.
- Profitability is evaluated with cost per ticket, support-assisted conversion, and downstream value. The speaker reports about **14% conversion** and **34% higher LTV** among customers who interacted with support, measured in a **72-hour attribution window** **[claimed]**; this design does not establish causality.
- Proactive delivery expectations and post-purchase communication are described as reducing ticket-to-order ratio **[claimed]**. The operating principle is to prevent avoidable contacts before optimizing agent speed.
- Custom integrations keep agents inside one helpdesk and automate replacements and quality-issue capture. The speaker says agents at other brands may spend **30–40% of their time** switching systems **[claimed]**; treat this as directional, not a benchmark.
