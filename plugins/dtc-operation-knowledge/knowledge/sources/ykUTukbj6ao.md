# The Secrets of Warehouse Inbound

- **Watch:** https://youtu.be/ykUTukbj6ao
- **Date:** 2023-09-14 · 10:57
- **Channel:** ShipHero

**Thesis:** Inbound is the control gate for warehouse truth: appointments, PO matching, identification, allocation, exception quarantine, replenishment, and cycle counting determine whether outbound can move.

## Transcript-grounded takeaways

- The inbound lifecycle starts before arrival: the customer creates a shipping plan/PO, the warehouse books an appointment, then verifies product and quantity when it reaches the dock (0:00–1:44).
- New inventory normally enters a sellable but unpickable overstock location; backordered quantity can be directed immediately into a pickable location so it does not wait for a later replenishment cycle (1:44–2:33).
- Staffing is planned against the inbound calendar and dynamically split among receiving, putaway, load balancing, inventory control, and replenishment (2:33–4:00).
- Inbound's operating goal is to keep outbound fed. The manager uses a replenishment queue under **10 items** as a local practice **[claimed]**, not a universal threshold (4:00–4:45).
- Damaged, mismatched, or unscannable inbound should move into an explicit non-compliance zone so it does not block the dock. This facility moves unresolved stock after two days **[claimed/local practice]** and follows up through the account manager (4:45–6:10).
- A flagged pick location becomes non-pickable until inventory control checks barcode, count, and replenishment; otherwise one bad location can hold orders without exposing the root cause (6:10–6:56).
- The manager attributes improved inventory visibility to designated zones, cycle counts, full audits, and explicit ownership rather than a one-time cleanup (8:27–10:07).
