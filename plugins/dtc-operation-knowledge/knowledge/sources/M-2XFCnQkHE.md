# How to Approach 3PL-Brand Partnerships

- **Watch:** https://youtu.be/M-2XFCnQkHE
- **Date:** 2023-05-01 · 22:55
- **Channel:** eCom Logistics Podcast

**Thesis:** A 3PL is an operating partner, not a pick-pack price sheet; fit, account management, planning inputs, escalation behavior, and continuous improvement determine whether the contract works in practice.

## Transcript-grounded takeaways

- The hosts say a majority of brand–3PL relationships end in separation **[claimed]**. The guest attributes breakdowns to poor fit, weak discovery, inaccurate forecasts, account turnover, and how both parties respond when inevitable exceptions occur (1:06–3:08).
- Selection is bilateral: channel mix, vertical, order profile, size, and operational capabilities must fit the warehouse. Lowest price is not enough if technology integration, management structure, or execution is weak (3:08–5:08).
- Governance should include a named account structure, proactive recommendations, continuous improvement, monthly KPI/SLA reviews, quarterly business reviews, and annual network review—not only reactive ticket resolution (4:10–6:10).
- Network design must consider inbound origin, ports, real estate, labor, outbound demand, parcel cost, and promised transit time together. A cheaper pick fee can lose to a worse total network (6:10–11:53).
- Node count is profile-dependent. The guest contrasts a **50,000-SKU** apparel/footwear assortment with a **300-SKU** cosmetics assortment **[claimed]** to show why duplicating inventory is not equally feasible (12:51–14:52).
- Automation should be justified through manual, semi-automated, and automated scenarios showing productivity, capex, labor, and payback; robotics is not inherently the right answer for every account (15:53–18:50).
- Dead inventory consumes cash, storage, and automation capacity. The speakers describe stock sitting five to seven years **[claimed]** and warn against sales teams repeatedly assuming old inventory will recover (18:50–21:50).
