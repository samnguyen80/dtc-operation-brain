# Ecommerce Contribution Margin Explained

- **Channel:** LedgerGurus: Ecommerce Accounting Experts
- **Watch:** https://youtu.be/e5r7-KT6mQ0
- **Date:** 2024-05-29 · 3:42

**Thesis:** Contribution margin is the money left after variable costs to fund fixed costs and profit; measuring it by business, channel, and eventually SKU prevents topline growth from concealing structurally unprofitable sales.

## Transcript-grounded takeaways

- Variable costs rise with sales and include COGS, fulfillment, shipping, payment processing, and advertising in the speaker's model; fixed costs include payroll, software, rent, and non-advertising marketing.
- Real cost behavior is messier than the labels: variable cost changes by SKU, bundle, weight, card, and ad conditions, while fixed cost increases in steps as capacity is added.
- `Contribution margin = revenue - variable costs`; `contribution margin ratio = contribution margin / revenue`.
- **[claimed]** In the speaker's example, a **$20** sale with **$14** of variable cost leaves **$6**, or a **30% contribution margin ratio**.
- Break-even revenue is `fixed costs / contribution margin ratio`; **[claimed]** the speaker's illustrative **$300,000** fixed-cost and **30%** ratio case gives **$1 million** of break-even revenue.
- Start with company-level contribution margin, then channel, then SKU when the data and decision value justify the additional work.
- Reliable contribution margin depends on accrual accounting and current costs; otherwise channel and product comparisons inherit inventory-timing errors.
