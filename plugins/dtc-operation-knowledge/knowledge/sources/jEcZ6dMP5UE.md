# How to Reduce Returns

- **Watch:** https://youtu.be/jEcZ6dMP5UE
- **Date:** 2022-06-06 · 5:01
- **Channel:** ReturnLogic

**Thesis:** Reduce avoidable returns by narrowing the expectation gap before purchase and feeding structured return evidence back into merchandising, marketing, product, and operations.

## Transcript-grounded takeaways

- A customer-centric returns policy may reduce purchase anxiety, although the video does not establish when added conversion outweighs abuse, processing, or reverse-logistics cost (0:00–1:01).
- Segment actual customer and return behavior instead of relying on a generic image of a "high-risk" customer; useful segmentation depends on data depth and consistent handling (0:00–2:01).
- Detailed visuals, descriptions, reviews, and fit information reduce the gap between what the shopper expects and what arrives. The assertion that inaccurate depiction causes **23% of returns** is **[claimed]** and uncited in the transcript (1:01–2:01).
- Capture feedback without making the customer navigate a burdensome process; the operational value is discovering product defects and process failures that ordinary sales data does not expose (2:01–3:03).
- Marketing and operations should use the same return signals: product-specific education can prevent predictable misuse or expectation mismatch after purchase (2:01–3:03).
- The Petco example reports products with reviews had a **20.3% lower return rate** **[claimed]**. The transcript gives no study design, category mix, or causal proof (3:03–4:06).
- Return reduction is continuous analysis across the whole journey, not a one-time policy rewrite (3:03–4:06).
